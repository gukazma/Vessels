# Pixel asset contract

Original deterministic artwork authored for Vessels. No downloaded asset pack or AI image service is used.

Art direction: a bright sunlit town, with warm cream masonry, terracotta / teal roofs, sandstone paving, blue-gray road shadows, lush layered green foliage, and restrained coral / gold accents. Small quarantine details carry the outbreak setting without a dark screen-wide tint.

- Ground: `street_ground.png`, 960 × 640, top-left world origin.
- Character: `survivor.png`, 128 × 160, sixteen 32 × 40 cells. Rows are down, left, right, up; columns are four gait frames. Column zero is also idle.
- Firearm stance: `survivor_aiming.png` uses the same grid, with raised arms held steady through all gait frames. `survivor_art.py` generates both sheets; empty hands and melee continue using the original walking sheet.
- Props: transparent PNGs, their node origin is the bottom center of each image. Collision shapes match the footprint rather than the full visible height.
- Rendering: nearest sampling, no mipmaps; draw at native resolution and scale the entire viewport by an integer.
- Editing: use any pixel editor, or adjust `tools/pixel/build_assets.py` and regenerate. The generator also rewrites `levels/quarantine_street.tscn`; preserve manual scene edits before rerunning it.
- Generators: `environment_art.py` for town surfaces and props; `survivor_art.py` for the four-direction character; `pixel_common.py` for shared helpers. `build_assets.py` combines them and authors the level. Each generator uses fixed seeds or explicit pixels, so repeated runs produce identical assets.

Only terrain is baked into the background. Buildings, trees, vehicles, fences and characters remain individual scene nodes so Y-sorting and collisions work independently.

`items/` contains six original 24 × 24 item icons and a 32 × 48 training target. Rebuild these with `python tools/pixel/build_items.py`. These icons are used in world pickups and the inventory.

`weapons/` contains dedicated 24 × 16 held sprites, pointing right (+X). Rebuild them with `python tools/pixel/build_held_weapons.py`. Each item resource specifies `held_texture`, `held_grip` (texture pixel coordinates, currently 6,9), and `held_tip` (muzzle or melee tip). The rig offsets the sprite by its grip before rotation/mirroring, so flipping cannot displace the hand. Keep these sockets in sync when editing artwork.

Firearms also specify `held_axial_texture` and `held_axial_tip` for front/back aiming. These narrow views show the top of the slide/barrel, without the side profile's grip and trigger guard. Their barrel center lies on y=9 through the grip. The view toward the camera uses foreshortening on the same pivot as the muzzle, keeping effects attached to the rendered tip.

The body hand sockets in `survivor_visual.gd` match the gloves and bob generated by `survivor_art.py`. Firearm sockets are down (1,-13), left (-10,-15), right (9,-15), up (1,-22), plus one pixel downward on odd gait frames. Melee sockets retain the original arm swing. Equipped characters face the aim direction while movement/dash facing is retained independently. `WeaponPose` is a child of the body Sprite; `show_behind_parent` masks rear-facing weapons and attack effects behind the body, without changing the player's world Y order or z index.
